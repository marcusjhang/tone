# Ship Korean color palettes, not confident verdicts

Korean "personal color" analysis (퍼스널컬러) is not a validated diagnostic science but a highly commercialized styling convention, and that distinction should shape every decision an AI-powered analyzer makes. It rests on four real color-science axes — warm/cool undertone, value (명도), chroma (채도), and contrast — inherited from Johannes Itten and Carole Jackson's *Color Me Beautiful*, then operationalized in Korea into a consumer industry of fabric draping, spectrophotometer skin readings, and printed result sheets ([Korea JoongAng Daily](https://koreajoongangdaily.joins.com/2022/12/17/business/industry/korea-personal-color-personal-color-analysis/20221217070018534.html)). What customers actually buy is a bundle, not a label: a compound type (e.g., "여름 쿨 뮤트"), best and worst palettes, and per-category prescriptions spanning fashion, makeup, hair, jewelry, nails, and glasses, delivered as a 결과지 and color cards ([COCORY](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=shH2koFA99k); [ifit](https://ifit.ai.kr/magazine/personal-color-16-types/)). The critical constraint is that the underlying measurement science is legitimate but narrow — peer-reviewed work shows national "season" systems disagree on what the axes mean, and consumer selfie pipelines carry color errors large enough to flip a season ([Cha & Kim 2011](https://doi.org/10.15810/jic.2011.7.4.002); [Niakan 2025](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39627966/)). No Korean AI app has published validated accuracy, yet the market is crowded and profitable, with free diagnosis as the hook and cosmetics commerce as the business ([매일경제](https://www.mk.co.kr/news/business/10769569)). The defensible product therefore outputs calibrated axis scores, palettes, and confidence — not a false-authoritative verdict.

## Korean personal color converts a Western convention into a commercial ritual

In Korean usage "personal color" carries two meanings: the innate body colors a person is born with (skin, hair, eyes) and the palette that best harmonizes with them ([Korean Wikipedia](https://ko.wikipedia.org/wiki/%ED%8D%BC%EC%8A%A4%EB%84%90_%EC%BB%AC%EB%9F%AC)). The theory traces from Swiss color theorist Johannes Itten, who split color into warm/cool and light/dark, through American stylist Suzanne Caygill's personality-linked method, to Carole Jackson's *Color Me Beautiful* in the 1980s; it reached Korea in the 1990s and went viral around 2022 on Instagram and TikTok ([Korea JoongAng Daily](https://koreajoongangdaily.joins.com/2022/12/17/business/industry/korea-personal-color-personal-color-analysis/20221217070018534.html); [Dr Rachel Ho](https://www.drrachelho.com/blog/personal-colour-analysis-seoul-korea/)). Korean practice is not a different theory so much as a finer-grained, more commercially aggressive operationalization of the same logic — the difference is resolution and application, not a separate color-science foundation.

The diagnostic vocabulary is a small set of near-orthogonal axes. Warm/cool is assessed as *undertone* (언더톤), the color tendency from dermal pigment composition, deliberately distinguished from surface *overtone* (오버톤); the canonical warnings are that pale does not mean cool, dark does not mean warm, and red skin does not mean cool ([skintonetest.com](https://skintonetest.com/tools/undertone-self-test/); [Postype](https://www.postype.com/@colorpicker/post/5174051)). In strict color science, **톤 (tone) = 명도 (value) + 채도 (chroma)**, independent of hue — yet in Korean beauty usage "톤" is used loosely as the label for the whole warm/cool-plus-subtype result ([Postype](https://www.postype.com/@pcolorlove/post/16170624)). The biological basis is dermal and epidermal melanin, hemoglobin, carotene, and light reflectance, which is why undertone is defined as a subsurface property that surface makeup and oil partly mask ([Korean Wikipedia](https://ko.wikipedia.org/wiki/%ED%8D%BC%EC%8A%A4%EB%84%90_%EC%BB%AC%EB%9F%AC)).

A standard in-person session is a carefully staged ritual designed to remove exactly the confounds a phone camera faces. Customers arrive bare-faced and without colored contacts; consultants don a white head wrap and cape and work in natural daylight roughly three hours after sunrise to three hours before sunset ([Korean Wikipedia](https://ko.wikipedia.org/wiki/%ED%8D%BC%EC%8A%A4%EB%84%90_%EC%BB%AC%EB%9F%AC); [Dr Rachel Ho](https://www.drrachelho.com/blog/personal-colour-analysis-seoul-korea/)). The consultant visually assesses vein, iris, and hair color, often takes a spectrophotometer reading reported as **L\*a\*b\***, then drapes fabric swatches across the chest and narrows 4 seasons to a subtype, with gold-versus-silver jewelry as a consumer-friendly warm/cool proxy ([COCORY](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=shH2koFA99k); [뷰티인사이트](https://beautyinsight.tistory.com/210)). A session runs about an hour and cost **₩50,000–150,000 in 2022**, with one 2023 report at **₩120,000** ([Korea JoongAng Daily](https://koreajoongangdaily.joins.com/2022/12/17/business/industry/korea-personal-color-personal-color-analysis/20221217070018534.html); [Dr Rachel Ho](https://www.drrachelho.com/blog/personal-colour-analysis-seoul-korea/)).

Crucially, Korea has **no government license** for this work. Certifications are 민간자격 (private qualifications) registered with the Ministry of Health and Welfare via the National Research Institute for Vocational Education and Training, issued by bodies such as the Korea Personal Color Association (KAPC) and the Korean Society of Color Studies, each defining its own subtype grid ([Naver blog](https://m.blog.naver.com/yeji2552/223748843694); [korea-edu.net](https://www.korea-edu.net/home/curriculum/info.php?item=473)). The official encyclopedia itself concedes results "can vary depending on the diagnoser's subjectivity," and reviewers report getting different seasons at different studios ([Korean Wikipedia](https://ko.wikipedia.org/wiki/%ED%8D%BC%EC%8A%A4%EB%84%90_%EC%BB%AC%EB%9F%AC)). A certification signals training completion, not a standardized or interoperable method.

## Four seasons anchor the system, but the subtype grid is a Babel of 8, 10, 12, and 16

The invariant backbone is the four seasons. Spring (봄웜) and Autumn (가을웜) are warm; Summer (여름쿨) and Winter (겨울쿨) are cool. Value and chroma then separate them: **Spring is high-value/high-chroma, Summer high-value/low-chroma, Autumn low-value/low-chroma, Winter low-value/high-chroma** ([Korean Wikipedia](https://ko.wikipedia.org/wiki/%ED%8D%BC%EC%8A%A4%EB%84%90_%EC%BB%AC%EB%9F%AC)). Everything above that four-cell grid is provider-defined. The subtype count ranges from 8 to 16 depending on institute and marketer, and the same consumer-facing label can denote different palettes across shops ([뷰티인사이트](https://beautyinsight.tistory.com/210)).

The **12-type system is the dominant consumer convention**, formed as each season times three subtypes: Spring as Light/Bright/True, Summer as Light/Mute/True, Autumn as Mute/Deep/True, and Winter as Bright/Deep/True ([Δme](https://deltame.app/blog/12-season-personal-color-types); [Korea JoongAng Daily](https://koreajoongangdaily.joins.com/2022/12/17/business/industry/korea-personal-color-personal-color-analysis/20221217070018534.html)). Naming drifts badly: 브라이트 equals 클리어, 스트롱, and 비비드; 뮤트 equals 소프트; 딥 equals 다크; and 트루 denotes the season's archetype ([Naver blog](https://m.blog.naver.com/0108383/222836971115)). Some vendors run **10 tones** — MyColor explicitly diagnoses "four seasons in 10 tones … based on the PCCS table" — while ifit and others publish **16 types** as four seasons times four axis keywords ([MyColor](https://mycolor.kr/en/explain); [ifit](https://ifit.ai.kr/magazine/personal-color-16-types/)). Korean guides add **간절기 (shoulder-season) hybrids** — 3월형, 6월형, 9월형, 12월형 — that straddle two adjacent seasons, plus a separate "image/골격" bone-structure axis ([뷰티인사이트](https://beautyinsight.tistory.com/210)).

There is no authoritative national registry or published subtype-definition document; the 12/16 taxonomies are practitioner-driven, not academic ([뷰티인사이트](https://beautyinsight.tistory.com/210)). The product implication is decisive: model the output as a **normalized object** — season plus a dominant axis (light, bright, true, deep, muted) — with a display-alias layer that maps 브라이트↔{클리어, 비비드, 스트롱} and 뮤트↔소프트, 딥↔다크. Default to 12 because it is what Korean media and consumers expect, but treat the label as a configurable view over continuous axis scores, not as ground truth.

## The product is the result sheet, and it is far richer than a season label

A Korean 결과지 is not one document but a bundle, and its sections define the natural output schema for an app. The headline is a compound label combining season, undertone, and sub-tone (e.g., "겨울 브라이트" annotated "청색/고채도/저명도"), usually with two to four image keywords and a one-line description ([yerimui blog](https://blog.naver.com/yerimui/224415306125)). Providers present it three ways: a definitive single type, a **ranked best/second/third list** ("1. 봄웜라이트 2. 여쿨라이트, 갈웜뮤트"), or a **position on two or three continuous axes** ([Naver café](https://cafe.naver.com/b00k2012/1603460); [Naver 지식iN](https://kin.naver.com/qna/detail.naver?dirId=8030302&docId=485204273)). Science-forward providers already show coordinates — Δme computes a three-axis octant and reports a mean color error of **ΔE 1.21**, while COCORY reports skin colorimetry as **L\*a\*b\*** ([Δme](https://deltame.app/blog/12-season-personal-color-types); [COCORY](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=shH2koFA99k)).

Beyond the headline, the sheet prescribes colors across nearly every consumer category. A representative mapping of what Korean providers deliver onto app fields:

| 결과지 section | What Korean providers deliver | App output field |
|---|---|---|
| Headline type | Season + undertone + sub-tone, image keywords, optional 2nd/3rd rank | `{season, undertone, sub_tone, label, confidence, secondary_types[]}` |
| Diagnostic evidence | Drape summary, L\*a\*b\* / octant / 명도·채도 | `axis_coordinates`, `measurement_confidence` |
| Palettes | Best, worst, and neutral color sets with names and swatches | `best_palette[]`, `worst_palette[]`, `neutral_palette[]` |
| Fashion | Tops/bottoms/outerwear, materials, patterns, contrast, 60·30·10 area ratio | `fashion{materials, patterns, contrast, area_ratio}` |
| Makeup | Foundation undertone, lip, eyeshadow, blush, liner | `makeup{foundation_undertone, lip[], eyeshadow[], blush[]}` |
| Hair | Dye colors plus a "헤어 레벨" (밀본 basis) | `hair{colors[], level}` |
| Jewelry / glasses / nail | Gold/silver/rose-gold, frame color and thickness, nail color plus art | `jewelry{metal}`, `glasses{frame_color, thickness}`, `nail{colors[], art}` |
| Products | Specific brands and shades, sometimes QR-linked or checked against the customer's own cosmetics | `recommended_products[]{brand, product_name, shade_code}` |
| Takeaway | Printed sheet, color card, chips, color book, PDF, mobile card | shareable card + exportable PDF |

Sources for these categories are consistent across providers. Clothing best/worst palettes are universal, with named hues and hex codes per sub-tone and explicit substitutes for avoided colors ([ifit 봄웜 guide](https://ifit.ai.kr/magazine/spring-warm-guide/)). Makeup is split into base/undertone, lip, eye, and blush — APL Color publishes foundation undertone guidance keyed to subtypes ([APL Color](https://apls.kr/resources/spring-personal-color-types-makeup-guide/)). Hair guidance includes a color and a level; jewelry reduces to a metal call (골드 vs 실버, with 로즈골드 common); glasses specify frame color and thickness; nail adds color and art design ([COCORY tone card](https://cocorymall.com/tool-personal-color/?idx=112); [luminousoliya blog](https://blog.naver.com/luminousoliya/224411819296)). Product mapping ranges from color-family-only at budget tiers to specific brand and shade at premium tiers, and COCORY's "립 파우치 분석" — analyzing the customer's own lip products — points directly at a high-value app feature: photo-scan owned products against the recommended palette ([soobin553 blog](https://blog.naver.com/soobin553/224338482679); [COCORY](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=shH2koFA99k)).

Representation is heterogeneous but the consumer expectation is stable: **swatch plus Korean color name is universal**, hex codes are increasingly standard among modern providers, PCCS is the stated theoretical basis for many studios, Munsell underlies the three-attribute model, and L\*a\*b\*/ΔE appear where actual measurement is claimed ([ifit 16-type guide](https://ifit.ai.kr/magazine/personal-color-16-types/); [namu.wiki snippet](https://namu.wiki/w/%ED%8D%BC%EC%8A%A4%EB%84%90%20%EC%BB%AC%EB%9F%AC)). Swatch counts are provider-defined, from COCORY's **52-card set** (fashion 12, makeup 16, hair 12, nail 12) and Δme's 13-patch card to commercial drape sets of 31, 64, or 87 ([COCORY tone card](https://cocorymall.com/tool-personal-color/?idx=112); [Δme](https://deltame.app/blog/12-season-personal-color-types)). Pricing tracks deliverable depth: online self-reports around **₩4,990**, group sessions **₩20,000–45,000**, 1:1 basic **₩80,000–160,000**, and image or wedding packages **₩150,000–400,000** ([Naver shopping](https://cr3.shopping.naver.com/v2/bridge/searchGate?nv_mid=91316596355); [컬러라이즈 listing](https://cr3.shopping.naver.com/v2/bridge/searchGate?nv_mid=12011932827); [Stylegrapher](https://www.stylegrapher.com/service_option/3)). An AI app naturally occupies the cheap tiers and can upsell physical cards.

## The market is offline-first and commerce-coupled, and the classifier is not the moat

Korea's personal-color market runs on a two-tier structure: a commoditized diagnostic core and a styling upsell. Offline 1:1 diagnosis anchors around **₩100,000–150,000**, with premium "total styling" packages reaching **₩540,000** and group sessions at **₩40,000–50,000**; COCORY's flagship session is **₩130,000**, and the top studios each report **1,000+ foreign visitors per month** ([매일경제](https://www.mk.co.kr/news/business/10989038); [KPI뉴스](https://kpinews.kr/newsView/179555630165354)). The foreign-tourism channel is material and margin-rich: Seoul sessions cost roughly **$80–160 versus ~$545 for a three-hour US session**, and Korea's beauty-service spend by foreign tourists surged **+231%** from 2019 to 2023 ([Time](https://time.com/6299099/personal-color-analysis-korea-tourism/); [매일경제](https://www.mk.co.kr/news/business/10989038)).

On the digital side, the app layer is crowded and consolidating. **Jamface (잼페이스)** reached **2.5M users**, outputs a 4-season type with subtypes, classifies **64 skin types**, matches against roughly **10,000** Korean skincare products, and monetizes through **in-app ads and a store**, not diagnosis ([매일경제](https://www.mk.co.kr/news/business/10769569); [조선비즈](https://biz.chosun.com/it-science/general_policy/2023/10/09/XLV4BNBEHVHODO63S34Y5AGC2Q/)). **Colorver (컬러버)** reports **800,000 downloads and 160,000 MAU** from a model built on **3,600+ analyzed products** ([ZDNet Korea](https://zdnet.co.kr/view/?no=20230223102403)). Behind them sit iColor, MyColor (10 tones), Clad, I-Fit, 발라랩, and 얼빛, nearly all taking a **single selfie or uploaded photo** and returning a season plus palettes ([Google Play iColor](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.ifamilysc.arda); [MyColor](https://mycolor.kr/); [Clad comparison](https://www.your-personal-color.com/blog/best-personal-color-apps-korea/)). Retail is becoming the dominant distribution channel: Olive Young's **SkinScan** has passed **1,000,000 cumulative diagnoses** and is adding a photo-based mobile personal-color service ("컬러핏") across **100+ stores**; a GS25 kiosk and a Toss in-app mini-app let users test without installing anything; Amorepacific's **톤워크** offers 600 options across 150 shades ([뉴스핌](https://www.newspim.com/news/view/20251229000135); [뉴시스](https://www.newsis.com/view/NISX20260915_0003789602); [KPI뉴스](https://kpinews.kr/newsView/179555630165354)).

Four business models coexist and blend. Paid offline B2C anchors on **₩130,000**; freemium apps give diagnosis away and monetize **ads plus affiliate commerce** (products tagged by personal color lifted Olive Young's April color-cosmetics sales **+56%** and lip makeup **+133%**); B2B retail embedding connects the store experience to online shopping; and education plus IP licensing monetizes the supply side, as with COCORY's academy and patent ([매일경제](https://www.mk.co.kr/news/business/10989038); [KPI뉴스](https://kpinews.kr/newsView/179555630165354); [서경대학교](https://www.skuniv.ac.kr/sku-today/59155)). The addressable prize is therefore better framed as **Korea's ≈₩3 trillion color-cosmetics market** than as diagnosis services; diagnosis is the acquisition wedge, and commerce is the P&L ([ZDNet Korea](https://zdnet.co.kr/view/?no=20230223102403)). Two strategic consequences follow. First, the defensible asset is **data plus product-catalog integration plus distribution**, not the classification model, since classifiers are commoditized and the biggest players already have scale. Second, the core ML idea is **already patented in Korea** — KR20100074412A uses fuzzy distance between input skin/hair/eye colors and predefined model colors to output a season, and KR102289628B1 uses a face image with a color model to select matching colors — so a new entrant needs a freedom-to-operate review ([Google Patents KR20100074412A](https://patents.google.com/patent/KR20100074412A/ko); [KR102289628B1](https://patents.google.com/patent/KR102289628B1/ko)).

## A phone camera measures pigmentation, not "season" — and the honesty tax is the opening

The measurement science beneath the industry is real; the typology built on it is not. **CIELAB L\*a\*b\*** is the standard perceptual color space and spectrophotometry is the "gold standard for color measurement," while the Individual Typology Angle (ITA) is a derived pigmentation metric from L\* and b\* ([npj Digital Medicine 2025](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41429926/); [Cole 2020](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32654351/)). But "undertone" is an **interpretive label on combinations of lightness, redness, and yellowness, not a directly measured physical quantity**, and ITA encodes only lightness and yellowness — it omits redness (a\*), so treating ITA as an undertone score is a category error ([Social Psychology Quarterly 2024](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39345779/); [Lipnick 2026](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41073884/)). The strongest evidence that "season" is a convention, not an instrument: a peer-reviewed study of **200 Korean women** diagnosed with UK, Japanese, Korean, and German drape sets found the systems weight different axes (Korea most by L\*, Germany by a\*), and that the Korean system assigned winter types the **highest b\* (yellowness)** — a result its authors note "does not follow the previous theories of personal color" ([Cha & Kim 2011](https://doi.org/10.15810/jic.2011.7.4.002)). **No peer-reviewed test–retest or inter-rater reliability study of seasonal color analysis was found**, and no study quantifies consultant agreement.

The photo pipeline compounds this with large, systematic error. Smartphones capture in an unknown RGB color space, which "prevents a standardized colorimetric skin analysis"; even adding a ColorChecker left a teledermoscopy system at **ΔE 1.8–6.6 (CIE76)**, with inter-smartphone variability of **ΔE 2.0–3.9** ([Cugmas 2020](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33142901/)). Consumer colorimeter apps measured forehead skin at **ΔE ≈ 7.6 absolute** versus a spectrophotometer, and since ΔE ≈ 1 is near the threshold of perceptibility while 3–5 is clearly visible, the measured color can sit several just-noticeable-differences from truth — enough to land in a different season bucket ([Niakan 2025](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39627966/)). Fixed-threshold ITA on uncontrolled images scored **22.9% accuracy against a 51.3% majority-class baseline** and failed on the darkest skin type, leading its authors to conclude ITA "should not be interpreted as a validated skin type measurement method" ([Skin Research and Technology 2026](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42578529/)). Skin tone is systematically encoded in imaging data and can confound AI models, and in a benchmark of **3,203 consumer selfies**, deep-learning models **underperformed a trivial constant-mean predictor** on localized features — a warning about where these tools fail ([J Biophotonics 2026](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41914466/); [Scientific Reports 2026](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42570980/)). Beauty modes and HDR tone-mapping alter a\* and b\* directly, destroying the signal. Unsurprisingly, **no validated head-to-head accuracy study of any Korean AI analyzer exists**; the field's own review concedes color selection still depends on "practitioner experience rather than standardized color-harmony criteria," and one Korean comparison blog states plainly that "전문가 진단이 정확합니다" (expert diagnosis is more accurate) ([Apartment Therapy](https://news.google.com/rss/articles/CBMieEFVX3lxTE1qT1pWcG5JRlhBcDZ2MHNCMnY4MkVWMTNmT1dzTXdJYktvUm44eUZNN1ozdUVHRkhRMzJiSWpaeFR5V284LVl6bnhNMDZYM1RiTlhfYWlFZ1k3M3BIaF9YaUZGOWczX0U0LTZCZ3ZSa3U1ek5DWWE3Yg?oc=5); [Naver blog](https://blog.naver.com/zomama/224224013167)). Korean practitioner sources themselves warn that digital images are "reference only" because screens and lighting vary and low-value pairing and contrast are hard to judge on a phone ([뷰티인사이트](https://beautyinsight.tistory.com/210)).

This is not only a technical problem but an ethical one. Colourism and skin-color anxiety are real and commercially exploited, and a tool that tells users their "wrong" colors can amplify insecurity and drive purchasing ([Frontiers in Sociology 2026](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42729134/)). The evidence supports a specific product posture. Calibrate or abstain: require a neutral reference such as a gray card or known-white object in-frame and refuse a season verdict without it. Report the physical basis — **L\*, a\*, b\*, ITA, and a confidence score** — rather than a confident label, and never map ITA alone to undertone. Guide capture explicitly (illuminant CCT around 5000–6500 K, no HDR or beauty mode, multiple frames, reject mixed lighting). Treat skin-tone equity as a launch gate with error metrics stratified by ITA and Fitzpatrick type, since thresholds that work on light skin fail on deeper tones ([Lipnick 2026](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41073884/)). And validate against a **physical colorimetric reference, not against consultants**, because the reference standard is itself inconsistent — a moving target no app can fairly be scored against. On top of that, the consumer-facing framing should say "styling suggestion," avoid color-psychology claims about mood or energy, add a non-medical disclaimer, and minimize retention of facial images as sensitive data.

## Conclusion

The most important reframe for this product is that Korean personal color analysis monetizes a **bundle and a commerce funnel**, not a classification. The season label is the headline, but the value customers pay for is the best/worst palettes and the per-category prescriptions for makeup, hair, jewelry, nails, and glasses — and the businesses that win attach those prescriptions to product catalogs and retail distribution. That means a new app should compete on the richness and shareability of its result output and on its commerce integration, not on a marginally better classifier, especially since Korean patents already cover image-based season selection.

The second reframe is that honesty is a differentiator, not a limitation. Every existing Korean tool takes a single selfie and returns a confident season while publishing no accuracy data; the literature shows those pipelines carry errors large enough to flip a type and that darker skin is at disproportionate risk. An app that requires a calibration reference, surfaces L\*a\*b\* and confidence, offers ranked and shoulder-season secondary types, and sells a paid human-review tier would address the exact complaints Korean consumers already voice — that results vary by shop and that apps are only references. The winning posture is measurement plus uncertainty plus a rich, optimistic result bundle: sell the palette and the styling, and let the user own the confidence, rather than selling certainty the camera cannot deliver.
